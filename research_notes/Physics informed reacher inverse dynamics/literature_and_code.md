# Physics-Informed Inverse-Dynamics Learning and Data-Efficient Tracking for a Two-Link Reacher

## Which foundational methods actually place this project in context?

### Takeaway

Deep Lagrangian Networks (DeLaN), Structured Mechanical Models (SMM), and differentiable Newton–Euler models are the closest foundations because they expose an inverse-dynamics map or a controlled mechanical model. Lagrangian Neural Networks (LNNs), Hamiltonian Neural Networks (HNNs), and their constrained/symplectic variants are important context, but most of their headline results concern unforced conservative rollouts rather than torque prediction and feedback tracking [DeLaN](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.04490) [SMM](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v120/gupta20a.html) [LNN](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.04630) [HNN](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2019/hash/26cd8ecadce0d4efd6cc8a8725cbd1f8-Abstract.html).

### Cited Findings

**Evidence-map labels.** **A — direct** means the method naturally predicts required generalized force/torque or was evaluated in robot inverse dynamics/control. **B — adjacent** means its mechanics prior transfers, but the published objective or state representation must be changed. **C — superficially similar** means it primarily addresses conservative forward rollout, latent-state prediction, or simulation rather than inverse-dynamics tracking. The labels are project-specific inferences from the cited papers, not labels assigned by the authors.

**The key model distinction is the object being learned.** DeLaN learns the rigid-body energy components `M(q)` and `V(q)` and derives torque structure; unrestricted LNN learns one scalar `L(q,qdot)`; HNN learns `H(q,p)` and requires canonical phase coordinates; DiffNEA learns physically constrained link parameters inside a known rigid-body algorithm; SMM allows energy and generalized-force modules to be separately known or learned [DeLaN](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.04490) [LNN](https://arxiv.org/html/2003.04630#S3) [DiffNEA](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v120/sutanto20a.html) [SMM](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v120/gupta20a.html).

### Inferences

- For a planar two-revolute-joint reacher with direct torque actuation, **DeLaN plus an explicit dissipative-force module** is the best neural centerline: it matches the manipulator equation, accepts ordinary joint coordinates, and gives the inverse map needed by computed-torque control [DeLaN](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.04490) [D-HNN evidence for dissipation](https://arxiv.org/html/2201.10085).
- If link geometry is assumed known, **DiffNEA or ordinary rigid-body parameter identification is a stronger small-data baseline than HNN/LNN** because its hypothesis class is much smaller and directly aligned with serial-chain mechanics [DiffNEA](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v120/sutanto20a.html).
- A basic HNN should be included only as a deliberately mismatched context model, if at all. Presenting it as the primary competitor would conflate canonical-state conservative rollout with robot inverse dynamics [HNN](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2019/hash/26cd8ecadce0d4efd6cc8a8725cbd1f8-Abstract.html) [LNN comparison](https://arxiv.org/html/2003.04630#S1).
- For two links, explicit Cartesian constraints are unlikely to repay their added machinery unless the research question specifically compares coordinate choices; generalized joint coordinates already encode rigid link constraints [Finzi et al.](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/hash/9f655cc8884fda7ad6d8a6fb15cc001e-Abstract.html).

### Gaps

- The foundational LNN/HNN papers do not establish data-efficient **inverse-torque learning under feedback tracking**; their strongest evidence is conservative forward prediction, so those results should not be transferred to computed-torque tracking without a new experiment.
- No primary source reviewed here proves that a learned positive-definite mass matrix alone gives closed-loop tracking stability under approximation error; passivity/energy structure and tracking stability are different claims.
- The papers use different data regimes, architectures, state coordinates, and metrics, so their reported sample-efficiency gains are not directly numerically comparable.

## What evidence exists for sample efficiency, extrapolation, inverse dynamics, and closed-loop control?

### Takeaway

The literature supports the qualitative proposition that correct mechanical structure can improve low-data generalization and control, but it also contains direct negative results for DeLaN on out-of-distribution robot data. The most defensible project design therefore evaluates both offline torque error and closed-loop tracking across explicit data budgets and distribution shifts rather than assuming that physical structure guarantees either one [SMM](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v120/gupta20a.html) [DiffNEA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08861) [Reuss et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13804) [Giacomuzzo et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ifacol.2023.10.1858).

### Inferences

- **Sample efficiency must be a learning curve, not one small-data point.** Use several logarithmically spaced trajectory or sample budgets, matched model capacity where possible, and confidence intervals over seeds; prior literature shows both large gains and severe DeLaN OOD failures depending on representation and task [Finzi et al.](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/hash/9f655cc8884fda7ad6d8a6fb15cc001e-Abstract.html) [Reuss et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.13804#S4.SS4).
- **Split by complete trajectories and excitation envelope.** Randomly mixing time points from the same sinusoidal trajectories leaks nearly identical states across train/test; the Reuss et al. failure appeared on unseen trajectories despite low training error [Reuss et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.13804#S4.SS4).
- **Offline torque NMSE and tracking RMSE answer different questions.** Report both, plus endpoint error and control effort, because low one-step error can coexist with poor OOD control and high feedback gains can hide feedforward deficiencies [DiffNEA](https://arxiv.org/html/2001.08861) [Reuss et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.13804#S5).
- **A fair minimal baseline set is:** analytic oracle, identified rigid-body/DiffNEA model, size-matched black-box MLP, DeLaN, and DeLaN plus explicit friction or a small residual. Basic HNN is optional context rather than a primary inverse-dynamics baseline [DiffNEA](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v120/sutanto20a.html) [Wu et al.](https://doi.org/10.26599/TST.2024.9010011).
- **Two links are an advantage for experimental rigor, not algorithmic novelty.** The project can afford dense phase-space diagnostics, repeated seeds, exact analytic checks of `M`, `C`, `g`, and controlled OOD tests that larger-robot papers often cannot afford [DeLaN components](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.04490) [Giacomuzzo et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ifacol.2023.10.1858).

### Gaps

- Within the primary sources reviewed, no paper isolates exactly this combination: a planar two-link reaching task, DeLaN-style inverse-dynamics learning, explicit training-data budgets, and computed-torque trajectory tracking against matched neural and physical baselines.
- “Number of samples” is not standardized: papers variously count random states, time steps, trajectories, or hours of robot data. A course report must define both the number of trajectories and effective state-action samples.
- Most published favorable energy-model results use clean simulated derivatives; fewer papers test noisy acceleration estimation, actuator dynamics, and distribution-shifted feedback tracking together.
- The 2026 torque-separation study is directly topical but simulation-only and does not provide an openly downloadable dataset in its data-availability statement [journal record](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/msd2.70048).

## Which code can be reused, and which repositories only look reusable?

### Takeaway

The author DeLaN repository is the shortest direct route and now includes both PyTorch and JAX examples, but it is a lightly maintained research snapshot. For a robust project, pair a locally tested DeLaN implementation with an actively maintained dynamics backend—JAXsim or MuJoCo/MJX—and pin the complete environment; treat the original LNN/HNN and archived PyTorch robot-model repositories as references rather than dependencies [DeLaN repository](https://github.com/milutter/deep_lagrangian_networks) [JAXsim](https://github.com/ami-iit/jaxsim) [MuJoCo/MJX](https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco).

### Inferences

- “Not archived” should not be reported as “maintained.” The DeLaN, HNN, and constrained-HNN repositories have sparse/no releases and stale code pushes relative to actively developed JAXsim, MuJoCo, dm_control, and Gymnasium [DeLaN API](https://api.github.com/repos/milutter/deep_lagrangian_networks) [HNN API](https://api.github.com/repos/greydanus/hamiltonian-nn) [JAXsim API](https://api.github.com/repos/ami-iit/jaxsim) [MuJoCo API](https://api.github.com/repos/google-deepmind/mujoco).
- For only two joints, a short independent implementation is easier to audit than a deep simulator dependency. The author repository is most valuable as an equation-level reference and regression oracle [DeLaN repository](https://github.com/milutter/deep_lagrangian_networks).
- JAXsim/MJX and a learned DeLaN solve different layers of the problem: the former provide differentiable known physics/simulation, while the latter identifies a structured unknown model from data. Calling either simulator a “physics-informed learner” would blur this distinction [JAXsim docs](https://jaxsim.readthedocs.io/latest/) [DeLaN paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.04490).
- `dm_control` and Gymnasium reachers are directly relevant task environments but only superficially similar methodological baselines; neither tests whether a learned inverse model is sample efficient [dm_control source](https://github.com/google-deepmind/dm_control/blob/main/dm_control/suite/reacher.py) [Gymnasium docs](https://gymnasium.farama.org/environments/mujoco/reacher/).

### Gaps

- The original SymODEN code repository could not be verified to a current, clearly licensed, maintained canonical repository during this pass; use the paper/OpenReview equations unless an author link is independently confirmed.
- Several research repositories have no tagged releases or lock files. A successful run on current hardware cannot be inferred from repository activity alone.
- JAXsim explicitly describes parts of its differentiability support as not thoroughly tested; this makes analytic/finite-difference gradient tests mandatory for any reported identification result [JAXsim docs](https://jaxsim.readthedocs.io/latest/).
- Repository state after 2026-11-24 is outside this snapshot.

## Which failure modes and negative findings should drive the design?

### Takeaway

The principal risk is prior mismatch: a conservative energy model can be internally consistent yet wrong for motor torque, friction, actuator dynamics, noisy derivatives, or unseen trajectories. Numerical conditioning and evaluation leakage are equally important; the design should make dissipation explicit, enforce/monitor mass-matrix conditioning, split by trajectory, and test closed loop at fixed gains [D-HNN](https://arxiv.org/html/2201.10085) [Reuss et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.13804) [DiffNEA](https://arxiv.org/html/2001.08861).

### Cited Findings

| Failure mode | Primary evidence | Design consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Conservation prior applied to a non-conservative plant** | Basic HNN explicitly conserves learned energy, while D-HNN reports that on data containing friction the HNN accrued roughly ten times the trajectory and energy MSE and even the plain MLP beat it [Sosanya & Greydanus](https://arxiv.org/html/2201.10085). | Add at least viscous/Rayleigh friction; if Coulomb friction, backlash, or actuator lag is present, add an explicit term or small residual rather than making `V(q)` absorb it [D-HNN](https://arxiv.org/html/2201.10085) [hybrid inverse model](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.13804#S3). |
| **Motor torque is not identical to ideal joint generalized force** | Reuss et al. separate rigid-body, sign-friction, and recurrent residual components specifically because rigid-body models cannot capture stick-slip friction, flexibility, hysteresis, and partially observed effects [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.13804#S1). Wu et al. likewise find a residual-augmented DeLaN more accurate than pure DeLaN on manipulator data [article](https://doi.org/10.26599/TST.2024.9010011). | State exactly what `tau` means in the dataset. In simulation, expose raw generalized torque; in MuJoCo, audit actuator gain/bias/dynamics instead of assuming `ctrl == tau` [MuJoCo computation docs](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/computation/). |
| **Canonical-coordinate mismatch** | The LNN paper demonstrates that HNN fails on a relativistic example when given non-canonical coordinates and explains that robot-like `(q,qdot)` need not equal `(q,p)` [Cranmer et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2003.04630#S5). | Do not feed `(q,qdot)` to a basic HNN and describe the comparison as architecture-only. Either compute momentum with a known/learned mass matrix or classify HNN as a mismatched contextual baseline [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2003.04630#S1). |
| **Singular or ill-conditioned velocity Hessian/mass matrix** | Unrestricted LNN must invert the velocity Hessian, uses a pseudoinverse to handle potential singularity, and incurs cubic scaling in coordinate dimension [Cranmer et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2003.04630#S4). | Prefer DeLaN’s Cholesky-positive mass matrix for this robot; use `solve`, monitor eigenvalues/condition numbers, train in float64 for diagnostics, and report the diagonal floor. Positive definite does not by itself mean well conditioned [DeLaN](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.04490) [LNN](https://arxiv.org/html/2003.04630#S4). |
| **Activation derivatives vanish** | LNN requires second derivatives; the authors note that ReLU is a poor choice because its second derivative is zero and found softplus best in their search [Cranmer et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2003.04630#S4). | Use smooth activations (`softplus`, `tanh`, or similarly twice-differentiable functions) in energy/mass networks, and test second derivatives numerically [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2003.04630#S4). |
| **Angle wrapping creates a discontinuous regression problem** | The augmented-DeLaN manipulator paper flags continuous revolute-joint angle wrapping and uses sine/cosine feature transforms as mitigation [Wu et al. PDF](https://www.sciopen.com/local/article_pdf/10.26599/TST.2024.9010011.pdf). | Encode each revolute angle as `(sin q, cos q)` unless joint limits guarantee a non-wrapping interval; keep the controller’s signed angle error definition explicit [Wu et al.](https://doi.org/10.26599/TST.2024.9010011). |
| **Acceleration labels amplify measurement noise** | Reuss et al. estimate acceleration from velocity using a forward-and-backward 5 Hz Butterworth filter; this is non-causal and was used for offline training [data collection](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.13804#S4.SS2). | Separate clean-simulation results from noisy-label experiments. If emulating hardware, add encoder noise and compare exact `qdd`, causal estimates, and offline smoothed estimates; never imply that a non-causal filter is available to an online controller [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.13804#S4.SS2). |
| **Random sample splits leak trajectory information** | DeLaN in Reuss et al. fit its small training set but failed on separate validation/test trajectories, with test normalized error 0.2718 versus 0.0086 for DiffBary [Table I](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.13804#S4.SS4). | Hold out whole trajectories, target locations, amplitudes, and frequencies. Include a clearly named interpolation split and at least one extrapolation split [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.13804#S4.SS4). |
| **More structure is not automatically better** | Giacomuzzo et al. found DeLaN degraded faster with increasing DoF than a GIP-kernel GP, despite DeLaN’s imposed Lagrangian structure [DOI record](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ifacol.2023.10.1858). Gruver et al. found that matching second-order structure and capacity can erase HNN’s advantage [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.04836). | Match parameter count/training budget, include a physical-parameter model and a black-box model, and attribute gains only after ablations isolate structure, dissipation, and capacity [Giacomuzzo et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ifacol.2023.10.1858) [Gruver et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.04836). |
| **Discrete integration can corrupt an energy-learning claim** | Marco David and Florian Méhats, “Symplectic Learning for Hamiltonian Neural Networks,” *Journal of Computational Physics* 494:112495, 2023, shows that the ordinary HNN loss can have an artificial lower bound caused by discretization and that a symplectic training integrator remedies it [DOI:10.1016/j.jcp.2023.112495](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2023.112495). | Keep the integrator and step size fixed across rollout comparisons, report them, and distinguish continuous-model structure from numerical energy drift [article](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2023.112495). |
| **Conserving a learned energy does not ensure accurate trajectories** | Gruver et al. show that HNN advantages decompose into Neural-ODE, second-order, symplectic, and conservation biases; integration error still accumulates and matched non-Hamiltonian models can perform as well or better [arXiv:2202.04836](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.04836). | Report state/torque/tracking errors in addition to energy drift. Never use near-constant learned energy as a surrogate for task success [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.04836). |
| **Physical components may not be uniquely identifiable from torque loss** | Reuss et al. explain that robot inertial regressors have dependent columns, motivating base parameters, and observe that different physically consistent solvers do not converge to the same inertial values despite similar torque prediction [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.13804#S2.SS1) [results](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.13804#S4.SS4). | Call `M`, `V`, and friction components “learned decompositions,” not recovered true physical parameters, unless excitation and identifiability are demonstrated. Compare total torque first; component errors are secondary diagnostics [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.13804#S4.SS4). |
| **Feedback gains can conceal feedforward-model quality** | Reuss et al.’s tracking errors fall substantially as gains increase; the authors explicitly use low gains to make tracking more dependent on the learned inverse model [control results](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.13804#S5). | Freeze identical gains for all models and report at least a low/moderate-gain condition. Tune gains once on a designated baseline or validation set, not separately on test trajectories [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.13804#S5). |
| **A recurrent residual can improve fit but weaken interpretability and deployment simplicity** | The hybrid Franka model uses an LSTM to represent hysteresis and partial observability, requires state warm-up, and trains on short sequences for stability [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.13804#S3.SS1) [control setup](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.13804#S5). | For a simulated two-link course project, begin with explicit viscous/Coulomb friction and a stateless residual. Add recurrence only if the plant deliberately includes hidden actuator state or hysteresis [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.13804#S3.SS1). |

**Minimum numerical and scientific checks implied by the evidence:**

1. Compare autodiff inverse torque against analytic two-link torque at random states; compare gradients with central finite differences [DeLaN equations](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.04490) [JAXsim warning](https://jaxsim.readthedocs.io/latest/).
2. Check symmetry, minimum eigenvalue, maximum eigenvalue, and condition number of `M_hat(q)` on train, interpolation-test, and OOD grids [DeLaN positive-definite construction](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.04490).
3. Report torque RMSE/NMSE per joint, total and component errors where identifiable, trajectory joint RMSE, end-effector RMSE, terminal error, peak/RMS torque, and failure/divergence rate [DiffNEA evaluation](https://arxiv.org/html/2001.08861) [Reuss et al. control metrics](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.13804#S5).
4. Repeat with multiple seeds and display distributions, because DeLaN’s reported OOD error had high variance in the real-robot comparison [Reuss et al., Table I](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.13804#S4.SS4).
5. Include an integrator/step-size sensitivity check for rollout claims and keep the controller sample time identical across models [David & Méhats](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2023.112495).

### Inferences

- The safest architecture for the proposed plant is `tau_hat = tau_DeLaN(q,qdot,qdd) + f_diss(qdot)`, with an optional small residual ablation. This preserves the direct inverse map while acknowledging the most repeatedly observed real-system failure [D-HNN](https://arxiv.org/html/2201.10085) [Wu et al.](https://doi.org/10.26599/TST.2024.9010011).
- A clean simulator can make an unrestricted physical model appear stronger than it will be on hardware. A useful course contribution is therefore a controlled degradation ladder: exact labels → noisy/filtered derivatives → friction mismatch → parameter shift [Reuss et al. data processing](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.13804#S4.SS2) [DiffNEA real-robot discrepancy](https://arxiv.org/html/2001.08861).
- “Physics-informed” should be treated as a hypothesis-class choice whose bias can help or hurt. The right conclusion is conditional on data regime, model mismatch, and test distribution [Giacomuzzo et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ifacol.2023.10.1858) [Gruver et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.04836).

### Gaps

- Existing papers rarely publish a complete condition-number analysis of learned mass matrices under OOD configurations, although positive definiteness is commonly enforced.
- There is little direct evidence on how DeLaN inverse-dynamics sample efficiency changes under realistic acceleration-estimation noise for a two-link tracking task.
- Closed-loop stability under learned-model error is generally evaluated empirically in this literature; formal robust tracking bounds would require a separate control-theoretic analysis.

## What is a defensible course-project contribution, and which claims would be overstated?

### Takeaway

A credible contribution is a careful, reproducible **comparative study**, not a claim to have invented physics-informed inverse dynamics: quantify how a DeLaN-style positive-definite inverse model with explicit dissipation changes the data required for offline torque prediction and OOD computed-torque tracking on a two-link reacher. Novelty can come from the matched experimental design, trajectory-level splits, closed-loop analysis, and connection between physical diagnostics and control outcomes [DeLaN](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.04490) [prior inverse-control studies](https://arxiv.org/html/2001.08861) [negative OOD evidence](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.13804#S4.SS4).

### Cited Findings

**Recommended claim, conditional on the eventual results:**

> “We present a controlled study of physics-structured inverse-dynamics learning for a planar two-link reacher. Under matched data, optimization, feedback gains, and trajectory splits, we compare an analytic/identified rigid-body model, a size-matched black-box MLP, DeLaN, and dissipation/residual-augmented DeLaN. We quantify learning curves for inverse-torque prediction and test whether offline gains persist in interpolation and out-of-distribution computed-torque tracking.”

This claim is appropriately scoped because DeLaN, SMM, DiffNEA, hybrid inverse models, and real-time learned-energy control all predate the project [DeLaN](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.04490) [SMM](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v120/gupta20a.html) [DiffNEA](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v120/sutanto20a.html) [DeLaN-4EC](https://doi.org/10.1109/IROS40897.2019.8968268) [hybrid inverse control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13804).

**A compact experiment that can support that claim:**

| Element | Defensible specification and evidence basis |
|---|---|
| Plant | Planar 2R arm with gravity and direct joint torques; run a conservative version and a version with known viscous/Coulomb friction because conservative-only results do not transfer automatically to frictional systems [D-HNN](https://arxiv.org/html/2201.10085). |
| Data | Generate persistently exciting joint trajectories with exact states/torques; report trajectories, duration, sample rate, and effective samples. Add a noisy-derivative condition modeled on the filtering issue in robot data [Reuss et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.13804#S4.SS2). |
| Budgets | Use several fixed trajectory budgets rather than one “small” set and repeat seeds; this directly tests the sample-efficiency proposition and exposes variance [SMM motivation](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v120/gupta20a.html) [Reuss et al. variance](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.13804#S4.SS4). |
| Splits | (1) interpolation in the same excitation envelope; (2) held-out trajectories/targets; (3) higher amplitude or frequency; optionally (4) changed mass/friction. Whole-trajectory OOD splits are necessary given published DeLaN train/test failures [Reuss et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.13804#S4.SS4). |
| Baselines | Analytic oracle; fitted physical-parameter model; MLP `(sin q, cos q, qdot, qdd_des) -> tau`; DeLaN; DeLaN + explicit friction; optional DeLaN + small residual. This separates known physics, architectural structure, and model mismatch [DiffNEA](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v120/sutanto20a.html) [Wu et al.](https://doi.org/10.26599/TST.2024.9010011). |
| Capacity/training controls | Match parameter counts approximately or report them; keep optimizer steps, early-stopping rule, normalized inputs/targets, precision, and seeds fixed. Matched complexity is necessary because apparent HNN gains can disappear under controlled capacity/second-order comparisons [Gruver et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.04836). |
| Controller | Use identical computed-torque/PD gains and sample time for every learned model; evaluate at low and moderate gains so feedback does not fully mask feedforward quality [Reuss et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.13804#S5). |
| Offline metrics | Per-joint torque RMSE and normalized RMSE; median and tail error; component errors only where identifiable; `M_hat` symmetry/eigenvalue/condition-number diagnostics [DeLaN structure](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.04490) [identifiability caveat](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.13804#S2.SS1). |
| Closed-loop metrics | Joint and end-effector RMSE, terminal target error, RMS/peak torque, settling time, and divergence rate; plot offline error against tracking error across seeds/budgets [DiffNEA tracking](https://arxiv.org/html/2001.08861) [hybrid control metrics](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.13804#S5). |
| Reproducibility | Commit code, lock exact dependencies, save seeds/configuration and generated datasets, and record wall-clock inference timing. Research reference repositories commonly lack releases, making project-level pinning important [DeLaN repository status](https://api.github.com/repos/milutter/deep_lagrangian_networks). |

**Claims that would be overstated, with safer replacements:**

| Overstated claim | Why it is not supported | Defensible wording |
|---|---|---|
| “We introduce the first physics-informed neural inverse-dynamics model.” | DeLaN, SMM, DiffNEA, and multiple hybrid inverse-dynamics models already exist [DeLaN](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.04490) [SMM](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v120/gupta20a.html) [DiffNEA](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v120/sutanto20a.html). | “We adapt and evaluate DeLaN-style inverse dynamics in a controlled two-link reaching benchmark.” |
| “Physics-informed models are sample efficient.” | Published evidence is mixed; DeLaN has shown severe OOD failure and worse scaling than a structured GP in some robot studies [Reuss et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.13804#S4.SS4) [Giacomuzzo et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ifacol.2023.10.1858). | “In our specified data and test regimes, model X reaches threshold Y with N trajectories versus M for the baseline,” with uncertainty over seeds. |
| “The network learns the true mass matrix, potential, and friction.” | Torque-equivalent parameterizations can be non-identifiable, and different physically consistent solvers can obtain different parameters [Reuss et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.13804#S2.SS1). | “The network learns a mechanically structured decomposition that predicts torque; we compare components to ground truth as diagnostics.” |
| “Positive-definite `M_hat` guarantees stable tracking.” | DeLaN guarantees a structural property of the learned mechanical model, not robust closed-loop convergence under approximation, delay, saturation, or discretization error [DeLaN](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.04490) [integration caveat](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2023.112495). | “`M_hat` is positive definite by construction; closed-loop tracking stability/performance is evaluated empirically under the stated controller.” |
| “The learned model conserves energy.” | Actuation and friction intentionally change mechanical energy, and a discrete integrator can introduce drift [D-HNN](https://arxiv.org/html/2201.10085) [David & Méhats](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2023.112495). | “The conservative component obeys the Lagrangian structure; we separately model work input and dissipation and report numerical energy balance.” |
| “The method generalizes to robot manipulators.” | One simulated two-link arm establishes only task-specific generalization; real manipulators add actuator dynamics, friction, flexibility, and measurement noise [hybrid inverse-control paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.13804#S1). | “The method generalizes across the tested trajectories/targets/parameter shifts on this simulated 2R plant.” |
| “DeLaN outperforms HNN.” | A basic HNN may receive non-canonical coordinates and lacks the reacher’s control/dissipation channels, making the comparison structurally mismatched [LNN discussion](https://arxiv.org/html/2003.04630#S1) [SymODEN](https://openreview.net/forum?id=ryxmb1rKDS). | Compare to a control-aware Hamiltonian model with valid coordinates, or describe HNN as contextual rather than a decisive baseline. |
| “Low test torque MSE implies good tracking.” | Published work shows OOD failures and strong dependence on feedback gains; one-step and closed-loop metrics are not interchangeable [DiffNEA](https://arxiv.org/html/2001.08861) [Reuss et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.13804#S5). | “We measure the empirical relationship between offline torque error and closed-loop tracking under fixed gains.” |
| “The implementation is real-time.” | Real-time is hardware-, batch-, precision-, and controller-rate-dependent; another paper’s 1 kHz result does not transfer to this code [Reuss et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.13804#S3.SS1). | “On hardware X, one control evaluation takes median/p99 Y/Z microseconds at batch size one.” |
| “The MuJoCo/Gymnasium Reacher validates inverse-dynamics learning.” | Those resources provide tasks and simulation, not evidence that a learned inverse map is data efficient [Gymnasium docs](https://gymnasium.farama.org/environments/mujoco/reacher/) [dm_control source](https://github.com/google-deepmind/dm_control/blob/main/dm_control/suite/reacher.py). | “We use a second simulator as an implementation-shift robustness test after training/evaluating on transparent equations.” |

### Inferences

- The most valuable likely result is not “DeLaN wins,” but a **regime map**: where physical structure wins at low data, where explicit friction is necessary, whether a residual closes the mismatch, and when offline advantages survive feedback [SMM](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v120/gupta20a.html) [Wu et al.](https://doi.org/10.26599/TST.2024.9010011) [Reuss et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.13804#S5).
- A negative result is publishable at course scale if it is controlled—for example, DeLaN matching MLP interpolation but losing OOD due to friction mismatch or poor conditioning—because comparable negative findings already matter in the literature [Giacomuzzo et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ifacol.2023.10.1858) [Reuss et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.13804#S4.SS4).
- The cleanest project title would emphasize **evaluation**: “Data-Efficient Physics-Structured Inverse Dynamics for Two-Link Reacher Tracking: A Controlled Study,” rather than implying a new general architecture [prior art](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.04490).

### Gaps

- The final novelty wording must depend on actual outcomes; no superiority, sample-efficiency, or extrapolation claim is warranted before the preregistered metrics and seed-level results are available.
- A simulation-only course project cannot resolve hardware effects such as gearbox friction, current-loop dynamics, backlash, flexibility, and encoder differentiation.
- Formal stability guarantees would require explicit assumptions and a robust/adaptive control proof beyond the empirical contribution mapped here.
